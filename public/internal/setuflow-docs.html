Product foundation

Product Overview

SETU Flow CRM is a multi-tenant commercial operating platform for businesses that sell, quote, negotiate, execute and close complex trade opportunities. The product is built to remove commercial ambiguity, enforce operating discipline and keep every participant aligned from capture to cash.

Why this exists
Platform

SaaS with organisation isolation

Each organisation has its own users, roles, routes, records, pipeline settings, quotes, orders, document profiles and workspace controls.

Primary users

Sales, Ops, Leadership and Admin

Sales converts demand, operations executes commitments, leadership monitors performance, and admins govern settings, data quality and policy boundaries.

Commercial model

Gate-enforced workflow

Quote sending, acceptance, order activation, dispatch and closeout are all bound by checks, approvals, status transitions and audit events.

AI posture

Assistive, never autonomous

Setu Guru drafts, explains and suggests. It does not silently act, auto-send or bypass human review.

✦
The design intent

We are building a premium commercial system that feels as intentional as a modern B2B product, but is operationally deep enough for real export and trade workflows. Documentation should communicate both the product vision and the working mechanics.

Product pillars

Core design principles

Traceability Workflow discipline Commercial immutability Operational clarity Mobile additive UX

Sent quote versions are locked. Orders trace back to accepted commercial intent. Every meaningful transition is recorded. Mobile helps field users capture and connect — it does not dilute the desktop workflow.

Business outcome

What changes when SETU Flow is used well

Leads become structured commercial objects. Quotes become governed versions rather than loose files. Order execution becomes stage-based. Leadership gets real pipeline and execution visibility. Teams stop guessing what happened and instead operate from shared evidence.

Commercial Workflows

The commercial workflow is the heart of the product. It must be explained as a disciplined lifecycle, not just a list of screens. Each stage changes both what the operator sees and what the system will allow next.

Lead to cash

Orders move through actual lines, document approvals, processing, delivery and finance handoff.

  • Stage events

Final invoice, payment and reconciliation confirm that the transaction is complete.

  • Zero-balance closeout
  • Archive readiness
  • Operational audit trail

Critical workflow rules

No quote send without clearance. No order execution without accepted commercial intent. No hidden AI action. No stage-skipping around approvals or document readiness.

Operator execution

Operator Guides

Instead of developer-heavy descriptions, these guides focus on how real operators work in the product. Each guide highlights what the user should do, what the system should show, and what must not be bypassed.

Human workflow

Guide 1 — Lead to quote readiness

Move from captured lead to a quote-ready opportunity without losing quality or traceability.

Sales
1
Open the lead command centerConfirm buyer context, source, organisation/account linkage and whether the record is still active.
2
Confirm product and coverageUse coverage or product-interest tools so the opportunity is structured, not a loose note.
3
Plan the next actionAdd or update the follow-up if the record lacks a clear date or operational owner.
4
Check quote blockersReview readiness, approvals or compliance gates before creating or continuing the quote.
5
Launch the quoteCreate the draft only when the lead is qualified enough to become commercial intent.

Do

  • Keep the lead structurally complete before quoting.
  • Use the CRM to record coverage and follow-up state.
  • Escalate blockers instead of bypassing them.

Do not

  • Quote from free-text notes alone.
  • Assume communication activity equals readiness.
  • Create disconnected quote records outside the governed path.

Guide 2 — Quote build, review and send

Create a trustworthy commercial proposal with correct terms, version control and send discipline.

Sales + reviewer
1
Add or verify products and quantitiesEnsure each line is explicit and commercially meaningful.
2
Set pricing basis and currencyConfirm incoterm, validity, basis, FX posture and supporting assumptions.
3
Resolve approvals or compliance issuesPricing exceptions and blocked postures must be reviewed before sending.
4
Send the quote from the systemThe send action should create the communication record and lock the version.
5
Record the customer outcomeAcceptance or rejection must be explicit so downstream actions remain accurate.

Guide 3 — Order execution and closeout

Execution begins with accepted commercial intent and ends only after documentary and financial closure.

Operations
1
Seed actual lines from the accepted versionKeep a clean separation between commercial intent and actual execution detail.
2
Approve document readiness before sendsDrafts can be previewed, but external sends should only happen once approved.
3
Track progress through stagesProcessing, delivery and finance should each write visible state changes and evidence.
4
Close only after invoice and payment confirmationOperational closure is a formal state, not a guess.

Role-aware access

Quick Reference

This section condenses the system into operational reminders for quick reuse. It is not the full workflow explanation — it is the fast cheat sheet for internal operators and reviewers.

Cheat sheets

Lead workflow reminders

  • Every meaningful lead needs product context and a next action.
  • Coverage should be structured, not hidden in notes.
  • Quote creation should follow readiness, not hope.

Quote discipline

  • Sent versions lock.
  • Pricing exceptions require explanation and approval.
  • Acceptance must be explicit before execution.

Order execution

  • Actual lines are execution records, not edits to the accepted quote.
  • Draft document previews are not external sends.
  • Closeout requires invoice, payment and reconciliation evidence.

AI and mobile

  • Setu Guru suggests — users approve.
  • Card scan improves speed but still needs human verification.
  • Mobile capture should feed the main CRM, not sit in isolation.

What actually works

  • The repo has a real business spine: catalog pricing, override reason, approval enforcement, accepted quote continuity.
  • Trade workflow, communications, integrations, and AI are all downstream of governed commercial truth.
  • The codebase contains real modules, not fake investor theatre.

What does not work well enough yet

  • Primary navigation under-exposes important work areas and over-hides operational modules.
  • The product still assumes the user understands the internal data model before the UI guides them through tasks.
  • There is too much “workspace” language and not enough direct task language.

What gets the biggest reaction in a live demo

  • Buyer concern: “Why am I switching between Leads, Pipeline, Quotes, Orders, Trade Events, Documents, and Integrations to do one job?”
  • Investor concern: “If the logic is this good, why does the product shell still feel partially internal?”
  • Immediate fix: reframe the app around one visible operating journey with clearer stage-based surfaces.

Module-by-module brutal review

Leads · 84%

2 PRs to 96%

What works: Lead records hold real business context and connect to product, market, compliance, and follow-up data.

What does not

  • The view asks the user to absorb too much before taking action.
  • Qualification is present, but the screen does not dramatize the next best move strongly enough.

Buyer confusion / investor worry

  • Buyer: “Am I qualifying a lead or reading a dossier?”
  • Investor: “Strong data model, but is the sales rep faster because of it?”

Path to 96%: compress the lead command center into a clear 3-lane layout: profile, blockers, next action. Surface “quote-ready / not quote-ready” as a primary state.

Pipeline · 78%

3 PRs to 96%

What does not

Path to 96%: add stage-specific next-step prompts, blockers, and one-click actions; reduce passive status exposure.

Investor and buyer verdict after this brutal pass

Would a serious buyer pay for this today?

Not at the current UX standard. A serious trade house could believe in the workflow logic, but they would still see too much friction between “I have work to do” and “the product makes the next step obvious.” They would ask for workflow cleanup before a broad rollout.

Would a serious investor fund this today?

Possibly, but on product potential more than current UX readiness. The investor story is strongest when the governed commercial thesis is front and center. The risk is not that the product is fake — it is that the shell and flow still under-express the quality of the underlying system.
